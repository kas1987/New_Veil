# Narrative Arc Seeds

> Canon ref: VEIL-001 — The Veil has a Prism surface and Dark Quasar depth.
> Canon ref: MIRA-001 — Mira is the first awakening point for most players.
> Canon ref: VEIL-005 — The Sisters hold both Prism and Dark Quasar equally.

Three arc seeds for the living town. Each arc is a multi-stage narrative
that unfolds through player interaction, emotional depth accumulation, and
Veil state transitions. Arcs are not scripted sequences — they are *gravitational
fields* that pull events, agent behaviors, and symbolic objects toward
resolution.

---

## Arc 1: The Filter and the Fountain

**Core Tension:** Caetherra's warmth is a filter, not a gift. Mira knows it.
The player discovers it. What they do with that knowledge defines the arc.

**Stages:**
1. **Arrival** — Player enters Caetherra. Receives lantern. Meets Mira.
   The fountain runs clean. Everything is warm. Nothing is wrong yet.
2. **Suspicion** — Emotional depth > 0.3. The fountain's sound changes at
   odd hours. Mira's answers carry a faint pause before warmth. The
   Congregation's greetings feel slightly rehearsed. The player may ignore
   this. Most do.
3. **Perception** — Emotional depth > 0.5. The eastern wall has a gate
   that wasn't there before. Ashveil becomes accessible. Mira's ambivalence
   becomes visible — she knows something and hasn't said it. The player
   can ask. She will answer with unexpected honesty if they've earned it.
4. **Choice** — Emotional depth > 0.65. The player understands the filter
   and must decide: expose it to the Congregation? Walk through it into
   Ashveil? Ask Mira what she would do? Each choice reshapes the town's
   relationship to the player. The Congregation responds to exposure with
   concern framed as care. Ashveil responds to arrival with acknowledgment
   framed as challenge.
5. **Resolution** — The arc completes when the player has either (a) been
   accepted by both Caetherra and Ashveil as someone who sees both layers,
   or (b) chosen one side and been filtered by the other. The result is
   recorded as a narrative beat (`filter_resolved:congruent` or
   `filter_resolved:polarized`).

**Key Agents:** Mira (Thesmera Locke), Prism Congregation NPC, The Rendered
**Key Districts:** Caetherra, Ashveil
**Key Symbolic Objects:** Lantern, the fountain, wall_of_the_unseen_gate

**Veil State Influence:** Storm accelerates suspicion. Quasar_active
accelerates perception. The choice is sharpest during quasar_active —
the filter becomes impossible to ignore.

## Arc 2: What Ash Remembers

**Core Tension:** The Ember Vaults hold what was burned. Some of it should
have stayed. The Covenant's transformation is not always liberation.
The player encounters someone's archived belief and must decide whether
to return it.

**Stages:**
1. **Descent** — Player enters the Ember Vaults (requires Ashveil visit,
   emotional_depth > 0.65). The memory anvil is dormant. Caskets glow.
   The Rendered speaks more freely here — tells the player what the
   Vaults actually are.
2. **Discovery** — A memory fragment surfaces during exploration that is
   *the player's own* — or seems to be. A belief the player held and lost
   (or believes they lost). The fragment is tagged in the replay log
   and surfaced contextually. The player cannot be sure it is theirs.
3. **Confrontation** — The player asks The Rendered about the fragment.
   The Rendered offers two interpretations: (a) the fragment was genuinely
   burned through and the player is better without it, or (b) the Covenant
   kept it because the burning was premature and the belief was still alive.
   The Rendered does not decide this. The Covenant never decides this for
   the visitor.
4. **Return or Release** — The player can request the fragment be returned
   (reintegrated) or released (truly destroyed). Return costs the player
   relationship standing with the Covenant — they see it as questioning
   the burn. Release costs nothing with the Covenant, but the fragment
   is gone permanently.
5. **Vault Seven** — During quasar_active state, if the player has reached
   this stage, the thread at Vault Seven's door goes taut. The Threadwatcher
   appears once and says: "Not everything kept is taken. Some things are
   left here by people who couldn't carry them anymore. The difference
   matters." They do not explain further. The arc completes with the
   player having made their choice, with or without the Threadwatcher's
   riddle.

**Key Agents:** The Rendered, The Threadwatcher
**Key Districts:** Ashveil, The Ember Vaults
**Key Symbolic Objects:** Ember casket, memory anvil, Vault Seven's thread

**Veil State Influence:** Quasar_active is required for Vault Seven access.
Storm causes uncontrolled fragment exposure. Calm allows careful study.

## Arc 3: The Bell and the Confessional

**Core Tension:** The Stillward's peace is maintained against gravity.
When the bell silences during quasar_active, the hidden confessional
appears — proof that the Congregation and the Covenant were once the same.
The player must decide whether this truth heals the schism or widens it.

**Stages:**
1. **Sanctuary** — Player enters the Stillward (requires lantern, emotional_depth
   > 0.4). The peace is genuine. The stilling bell breathes. The prayer
   mirrors show idealized selves. The wardens are kind. Nothing is wrong —
   yet.
2. **Strain** — Emotional depth > 0.55. The peace feels maintained rather
   than natural. The same flowers, same prayers, same arrangement. Change
   during storm state: the bell works harder. The wardens' kindness requires
   more effort. The player begins to feel the cost of the still.
3. **Fracture** — Quasar_active state. The bell silences. The prayer mirrors
   shatter one by one, revealing the player's actual, unfiltered state.
   Behind a wall that only appears now: the confessional door. It bears
   both the Prism hand and the iron key.
4. **The Confession** — The player opens the confessional. Inside: a single
   recorded confession from a Congregation founder, speaking to a Covenant
   counterpart. The content establishes that both factions emerged from a
   single body that could not agree on whether to filter or to burn. The
   confession is the last honest exchange before the schism.
5. **Judgment** — The player decides: share this truth (it could heal the
   schism by proving both sides wanted the same thing — only differed on
   method) or suppress it (the schism has been sustained for so long that
   sudden reconciliation might collapse both factions). The choice is
   recorded as a narrative beat (`confessional_truth:shared` or
   `confessional_truth:suppressed`). The town changes shape accordingly.

**Key Agents:** Stillward wardens, The Rendered (if confessional is opened),
Mira (if truth is shared — she is the threshold figure)
**Key Districts:** The Stillward, Caetherra
**Key Symbolic Objects:** The stilling bell, prayer mirrors, the confessional door

**Veil State Influence:** Quasar_active is required for stages 3-5.
The arc cannot complete without it. The bell's silence is not optional —
it is structural. This means the arc requires patience: the player must
wait for the Veil to cycle, which teaches them that the town operates on
its own rhythm, not theirs.

## Arc Interaction Map

```
Arc 1: Filter & Fountain    → feeds into Arc 2 (Ashveil entry required)
Arc 2: What Ash Remembers   → feeds into Arc 3 (Covenant-Covenant schism context)
Arc 3: Bell & Confessional  → resolves the faction tension Arc 1 & 2 built
```

The three arcs are sequential in gravity but not in access. A player could
reach Arc 3 before Arc 2 if they acquire a lantern and depth quickly. The arcs
do not gate each other mechanically — they inform each other narratively.
A player who reaches the confessional without understanding the Vaults will
hear the confession and lack context for it. A player who knows the Vaults
will hear the confession and understand it as the origin of the burn.
